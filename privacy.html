Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

This policy explains what the game Grandpa Runs Sidewalk Splat (the "game") collects and why. The game is made and published by Christopher Hall under the name Oak Street Games. "We" means Christopher Hall, publishing as Oak Street Games, who decides how the game's data is used. Questions or requests: twocrazymojo@yahoo.com.

The short version

  • You can play without an account and without sharing anything.
  • Gameplay statistics are short summaries of how levels go, such as where runs end, so we can improve the levels, their difficulty and the in-game shop. They carry a random ID that changes every day, never your name. The game asks first, and you can turn them off at any time.
  • If you buy shards, Apple handles the payment. We keep a record of the purchase so your shards are credited exactly once. That record is kept apart from gameplay statistics.
  • No ads, no advertising or tracking tools, and we do not sell or share your data with advertisers or data brokers.

Gameplay statistics (optional)

This section describes version 2.0 of the game and later. If you still use version 1.0.0, see Version 1.0.0 below.

Gameplay statistics help us improve the levels, their difficulty and the in-game shop. The first time you open the game it asks whether to share them (Share Analytics or No Thanks). Nothing is collected before you choose, and nothing is collected if you choose No Thanks. This is the same in every country. An answer you already gave with an earlier version (Share Analytics, No Thanks, or turning the old switch off) is kept. You can change your choice at any time in User Settings on the title screen (Share Gameplay Statistics). The game plays exactly the same either way, and your rewards and purchases do not depend on it.

If you share, the game sends:

  • One summary for each level attempt: which level and which version of it, whether you started from the beginning or from a checkpoint, how the attempt ended (a win, a knockout, a restart, quitting, or the app closing), how long you played it, how far you got, how many hearts you lost in each tenth of the level, what took your last heart (for example "a falling poop near the end of Level 2"), and for a win its time and medal;
  • How long you play: minutes with the app open and minutes actually playing, and whether this is the first time this copy of the game has sent anything;
  • Shop spending: which outfit, outfit set or bonus hearts you bought with shards, the price, how many of those shards you earned in the game and how many you bought, and whether it went through;
  • the app's version and build, whether it came from the App Store or a test build, and the date on your device.

The daily ID. Each summary carries a random ID that your device makes up each calendar day and deletes when the day changes. It is not made from your device, your IP address, your Apple Account or anything else, and it is not the ID used for purchases or an advertising identifier. It lets us count how many copies of the game were played on a day and connect that day's summaries to each other. It does not let us connect one day to another, so we cannot tell whether you came back. Data with a daily ID is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it does not name you, but it is still treated as personal data.

Not in the summaries: your name, email address, contacts, location, photos, microphone, anything you type, exact times, your device model, operating system version or screen size. (Our host's request logs are different: see "Where it goes".)

Why: to see which levels, checkpoints and obstacles are too hard or too easy, how much the game is played, and how the shop is used, so we can fix and improve the levels, their difficulty and the in-game shop. We never use it for advertising, to contact you or to build a profile of you.

Where it goes: our own database, hosted for us by Supabase in Canada (on Amazon Web Services; Supabase's network provider, Cloudflare, handles the connection). Like any website, our host sees your IP address when the game connects. Our database does not store it: for less than half an hour we keep a scrambled, one-way version of it to stop floods of fake data, and each summary is stored with the date it arrived. Supabase's own request logs keep, for 7 days, the IP address, an approximate location worked out from it (such as country, region, city and postal code), the kind of device and its iOS version as the game's web view reports them, and technical details of the connection, for security and fixing problems. These logs do not hold the summaries or the daily ID; we do not copy them into our database or use them to identify players. This applies to every connection the game makes to us, including purchases.

AI tools: we may use an AI tool, such as ChatGPT, to help us understand our statistics. The review files we prepare for this hold daily totals with no IDs (for example "Level 2, Tuesday: 140 attempts, 61 wins"), not the summaries themselves.

How long: summaries with a daily ID are deleted 35 days after their day. Before that they are counted into daily totals that contain no ID at all (for example how many attempts Level 2 had on a day); those totals are deleted after 25 months.

Stop and delete: turn Share Gameplay Statistics off in User Settings. Collection stops at once: the summaries not yet sent and today's ID are deleted from your device, and the game stops waiting for any send still under way. Turning it off does not delete anything our server already received (a send under way may already have arrived). To delete what was already sent, note today's statistics ID shown in User Settings while sharing and email it to us; we will delete that day's summaries and recount the totals without them. (After 35 days they are already gone.)

Version 1.0.0

Version 1.0.0 of the game asks the same question first (Share Analytics or No Thanks), and collects nothing unless you choose Share Analytics. If you do, it sends more detailed gameplay data than version 2.0: each gameplay event (for example each hit, with where in the level it happened), visits and play time, cutscenes, the shop screens you opened, frame-rate summaries, the device model, iOS version and screen size, and one random analytics ID that stays the same until you turn sharing off or delete the app. It goes to the same database and through the same host as described above, and is kept for 400 days after it arrived, then deleted. To stop it, turn Share Gameplay Analytics off in User Settings: collection stops and the data not yet sent and the analytics ID are deleted from your device. To delete what was already sent, note the Analytics ID shown in User Settings while sharing and email it to us.

Updating to version 2.0 stops this detailed data: the update deletes any of it not yet sent, and never sends it. If you shared with version 1.0.0, version 2.0's User Settings still shows that earlier analytics ID while you share, so you can ask us to delete what version 1.0.0 sent; turning sharing off deletes the ID from your device.

Purchases (100 Poop Shards)

Payments are processed by Apple; we never see your card or Apple Account details. To credit your shards exactly once and keep your balance, our wallet server keeps: Apple's transaction ID, the product, the purchase date, the environment (test or real), a random wallet ID created on your device (also sent to Apple with the purchase), a one-way hash of a random device secret, your shard balance and what you spent bought shards on. Apple may notify us of refunds; we record them. This is needed for purchases to work, so it is not optional, and it works the same whether or not you share statistics. It is kept in the same Supabase database in Canada, separately from gameplay statistics, and is never connected to them or used for advertising. To see how the shop is doing, we look at daily totals from these records (for example how many packs were bought on a day), with no IDs. We keep purchase records while the game offers purchases, so bought shards stay credited, and afterwards for as long as our accounting and tax records require. They are not deleted automatically: you can ask us to delete yours, and we will keep only what those records require. Bought shards do not expire.

Update check

From version 2.0.1, when the game starts, and when you come back to it, it asks our server whether this version can still be played or an update is available. The request carries no ID, no game data and none of your choices. Our host's request logs keep it for 7 days like any other connection the game makes (see "Where it goes" above). The game keeps the last answer on your device so it still applies for up to 7 days without a connection; without one for longer, it lets you play. Updating never changes your progress, shards or outfits.

Rating prompt

From version 2.0, after you finish Level 2, the game may ask Apple, once, to show its standard rating prompt. Apple decides whether it appears. We do not learn whether it appeared or what you chose, and the game keeps only a note on your device that it asked, so it never asks again.

Data stored only on your device

Your progress, best times, medals, earned shards, costumes and settings, your statistics choice, a note of whether this copy of the game was first installed with version 2.0 or earlier, the statistics waiting to be sent with today's ID, the earlier analytics ID (if you shared with version 1.0.0), the rating-prompt note and (from version 2.0.1) the last update-check answer and any optional update you put off are stored on your device, in the game's own storage. Deleting the game deletes them; a new installation afterwards starts as new.

Children

The game is made for a general audience. It is not designed or marketed specifically for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Gameplay statistics never include a name or contact details. They are used only for the game's internal operations: to analyze how the game works so we can improve its levels, difficulty and shop, and to send them to our server (which sees the device's IP address). We make sure the daily ID and the IP address are never used to contact anyone, to build a profile of anyone, for advertising or for any other purpose: the ID changes every day and is never linked to any other ID, the game contains no advertising or third-party analytics code, our database never stores the IP address, and summaries are deleted after 35 days. If you believe a child has shared statistics, turn them off in User Settings and email us today's statistics ID (in version 1.0.0, the Analytics ID) and we will delete that data. Purchases can be blocked with Screen Time.

Your choices and rights

You can stop sharing at any time in User Settings. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what data we hold about you, to correct or delete it, to object to its use or to take back your consent (which does not affect what happened before). Email us with today's statistics ID or the earlier analytics ID (for statistics) or the date of your purchase and Apple's order ID (for purchase records). We use gameplay statistics only with your consent (Share Analytics), and purchase records because we need them to provide what you bought. Our host's request logs serve the security and running of the service. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes

We will post any changes on this page with a new date. If a version of the game ever shares statistics by default somewhere instead of asking first, we will update this page before that version is released, and the game will tell you and show how to turn them off.
